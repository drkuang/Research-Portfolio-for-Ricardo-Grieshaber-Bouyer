01 / Profile

Research Profile

I hold a Master's degree in Immunology from Peking University (June 2026). My work combines computational systems immunology with experimental immunology, using bulk and single-cell transcriptomics, spatial analysis and multi-omics to investigate immune-cell states across tissues and disease contexts.

My experimental background includes primary T-cell isolation and activation, lentiviral transduction, CAR-T expansion, CRISPR-based functional screening, flow cytometry and mouse models. Alongside tumour-immunology research, I have contributed to inflammatory-disease multi-omics collaborations, including neutrophilic asthma. I aim to connect patient-linked immune profiling with functional experiments to understand disease heterogeneity and therapeutic response.

04 / Research alignment

Why the Grieshaber-Bouyer Lab

I am particularly drawn to the laboratory's translational systems-immunology approach: using high-dimensional patient data to define immune-cell states, then testing disease-relevant mechanisms with in-vitro systems, CRISPR-mediated perturbations and animal models. I am especially interested in the group's work on neutrophil heterogeneity across tissues, time and inflammatory conditions, and in understanding how these cellular programmes relate to disease variation and treatment response.

I could contribute computational experience in R/Python, bulk and single-cell RNA-seq, TCR-seq, spatial transcriptomics and multi-omics integration, including cell-state annotation, differential programmes, cell–cell communication and cross-dataset comparisons. My collaborative work involving neutrophilic asthma multi-omics, together with single-cell and spatial analyses in other disease settings, has strengthened my interest in extending systems-level approaches beyond cancer and into immune-mediated disease.

I can also contribute experimental experience in primary immune-cell handling, flow cytometry, CRISPR-based functional screening, lentiviral transduction and mouse models. In particular, my T-cell engineering and functional-assay experience provides a useful foundation for studying cell-directed therapies. I am excited by the laboratory's work translating T-cell redirecting approaches into treatment strategies for autoimmune disease, and would welcome the opportunity to develop my expertise in patient-sample-based clinical immunology and functional validation.
